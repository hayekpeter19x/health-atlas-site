How the Atlas score works

One number from 0 to 100, built from nine areas. Here is exactly how it adds up.

Nine areas

Each area has a short list of basics: habits you answer questions about, and results you can add (blood tests, blood pressure, Apple Health numbers). An area's score is the share of its basics you have covered, from 0 to 100. A blood result counts in full when it is in the optimal range, a little over half when it is in the normal range, and only a little when it needs work.

How much each area counts

The Atlas score is the average of your area scores, with some areas counting more than others.

When an area has no score yet

An area you have not answered enough about is left out, and the others share its weight. The app says so: "Based on 7 of 9 areas". Adding a blood test or connecting Apple Health fills in the areas that need them.

Where the targets come from

Targets follow the American Heart Association, the American Diabetes Association, the US Preventive Services Task Force and the CDC. The "optimal" ranges are stricter than the normal ranges printed on a lab report.

Atlas, the benchmark

Atlas is a benchmark built from published lab averages of one very thoroughly tested person. Where lower or higher is simply better, the better number wins. Where there is a healthy range, a result inside the optimal range beats one outside it, two results inside it tie, and one too low against one too high is a tie. A test Atlas has no published value for takes no part. Health Atlas is not affiliated with that person.

Challenge levels

Levels for the fitness challenges come from published research where it exists. Where a level is our own estimate, the app marks it "est."

What the score is not

The Atlas score is a guide for general wellness. It is not a diagnosis, a risk estimate or medical advice, and it does not replace your doctor.
