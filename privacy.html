vs Atlas Privacy Policy

Effective November 16, 2026. vs Atlas was called Health Atlas until November 2026; only the name changed. Short version: your health data stays on your phone, apart from what you choose to share with friends.

What’s stored

Your answers, profile (with your profile photo, if you add one), lab results, to-dos and score history are saved only in the app’s storage on your phone. Your photo leaves your phone only on a challenge picture you choose to send and, as a small copy, through your optional account for friends who added you back (below). None of this is sent to us or anyone else, except what your optional account shares with friends (below).

Your account (optional)

You can use every part of vs Atlas without an account. If you create one, it keeps your email address, your password if you chose one (stored scrambled, so no one can read it), your first name and your challenge records: your best times and reps, which of them you filmed, and how many tries you did in the last 90 days. It also keeps your link ID (the code in your challenge links) and the link IDs of the friends you’ve added, so your records and those friends come back when you sign in on a new phone. It also keeps the link IDs of people you’ve blocked (never their names), so they get nothing of yours. A cheer or an emote you send a friend who added you back is kept for 30 days (who sent it, which one, for which challenge, and when), so they can see it. Friends who already have your challenge link and an account can see your latest records and first name without a new link; you can switch that off in the app under You → Your account. If you say yes (the app asks once, and You → Your account has a switch, “Blood results”), it also keeps your latest blood results (never Apple Health or Health Connect data), so friends can compare them with theirs. Unless you switch it off (You → Your account → “Record videos”), it also keeps your record videos: your best filmed try for each challenge, replaced when you beat it. Nothing goes up until a friend has added you back (you added each other). Only you and friends who added you back can see them; videos are in a private store and play through links that expire after an hour. They’re never used for ads or anything else, and switching off, deleting a video or deleting the account removes them. While a friend has added you back, it also keeps your area scores (the nine numbers of your health map, not your answers), and a small copy of your profile photo, both seen only by friends who added you back; switch them off under You → Your account → Health map and Photo. It also keeps your Atlas score for the worldwide leaderboard, where everyone with an account is ranked: other players see your username, or for an account that has none yet the first two letters of the name followed by stars (like “Pe***”), your score and a blurred initial, never your photo or anything else. In the same way, your best try in the week’s challenge is shown on the Leaderboard (your username and the result) and is kept for eight weeks; switching the worldwide leaderboard off takes you off it. If a filmed try of yours finishes a week in its top three, the medal you won (the week, the challenge, your place and your result) is kept with your account and shown on your profile to people with an account, until you delete your data or your account; switching the worldwide leaderboard off hides it. You can leave the leaderboard any time under You → Your account → Worldwide leaderboard. With an account, the name you chose in the app is your username. It is public: other people with an account can find you by it when they add a friend, and it shows on the leaderboard. If someone already has that name, you choose another. You can change it any time under You → Your account → Username. It never holds your answers, Apple Health or Health Connect data. If you continue with Apple or Google instead of a password, Apple or Google only confirms it’s you and shares your email address (with Apple you can hide it behind a relay address that forwards to you). We never see your Apple or Google password. The account is stored with Supabase (our account service, servers in the United States), and the emails with your sign-in and password-reset codes are sent through Resend. They handle it only to provide that service to us. Only you can read your account; delete it any time, in the app (You, tap your email under your name, then Delete my account) or at vsatlas.app/delete.html, which removes it and everything in it.

Apple Health

On iPhone, if you connect Apple Health, vs Atlas reads steps, exercise minutes, sleep, heart rate, heart rate variability, VO₂ max, blood pressure, weight, height and body fat. It uses them only to show your scores and plan on your iPhone. vs Atlas never writes to Apple Health, and this data is never sent off your device, shared with anyone, used for advertising or stored in iCloud by the app. You can turn access off at any time in Settings → Health → Data Access & Devices → vs Atlas.

Health Connect

On Android, if you connect Health Connect, vs Atlas reads steps, sleep, resting heart rate, heart rate variability, VO₂ max, blood pressure, weight, height and body fat. It uses them only on your phone, for the challenges and screens that show them. vs Atlas never writes to Health Connect, and this data is never sent off your device, shared with anyone or used for advertising. You can turn access off at any time in your phone’s Health Connect settings.

Pro: your health score

Pro adds the health questions, your Atlas score, your Health map and your to-dos. Your answers, your score and your to-dos are saved only on your phone. If you turn on “Share my score with friends”, your Atlas score and area scores are kept with your account for friends who added you back, and go on the challenge links you send. Turn it off and they are cleared from your account.

Ask Atlas

On iPhone, questions you ask Atlas are answered by Apple Intelligence running on the phone. Your question and your data stay on the phone and are never sent to us or anyone else.

Friends and challenges

When you send a challenge, the link you share carries your first name, your Atlas and area scores and your challenge records (which of them you filmed, but never the video itself, and how many tries you did in the last 90 days), plus your lab results only if you switch that on. A link for one challenge leaves your Atlas and area scores out. You send it yourself, through Messages or any app you choose. To keep links short, your phone locks your card (AES-256) with a 10-character code that exists only in the link, and our server keeps just the locked copy, found by a fingerprint of the code, for 90 days. We never see the code, because it sits after the “#”, a part of a link browsers never send to a website, so we can’t unlock or read your card. Your friend’s app, or this website’s challenge page (c.html), fetches the locked copy and unlocks it with the code from the link. The page has no analytics. If you choose to send a picture with the challenge, it shows your first name, your result and your profile photo if you added one; it goes only where you send it. Your challenge attempts and the friends’ cards you add are saved only on your phone.

Challenge videos

When “Video” is on for a challenge (it is unless you switch it off for that challenge), the camera is used only while that challenge runs. No sound is recorded. While it films push-ups, the app counts them by finding your shoulders, elbows and wrists in the picture. That happens on your phone: no picture and no body position is sent anywhere for it, and none is kept. The video is made on your phone with your first name, the challenge, the date and the timer on it, and your best filmed try for each challenge is kept on your phone as your record video, until a better filmed try replaces it or you delete it. It goes only where you choose to send or save it, and, if you have an account, to your account for friends who added you back, unless you switch that off (see above). Otherwise we never receive it.

Lab reports and screenshots

A lab report you import is read on your phone: a PDF on iPhone and Android, and on iPhone also a screenshot or photo (with Apple’s on-device text recognition). Only the numbers you confirm are saved, and the file isn’t kept or uploaded.

Apple Health files

When you import an Apple Health export, the file is read on your iPhone. Only the numbers you choose are saved in the app. The file itself isn’t kept or uploaded.

No tracking

No ads, no analytics, no third-party trackers, and no data sold or shared. We can’t see what you enter in the app.

Backups

Like other app data, vs Atlas data can be included in your own iPhone or iCloud device backup, which Apple encrypts and only you can restore. On Android, the app’s data is left out of device backups. You can also make a backup file yourself (You → Back up my data) and save it where you choose, such as Files, iCloud Drive or Google Drive. It holds everything in the app, so keep it somewhere private. We never receive it.

Deleting your data

In the app, go to You → “Delete everything and start over”, or delete the app. Either one removes all your data from the phone for good. With an account, “Delete everything and start over” also clears what the account keeps about you. To remove the account itself, delete it in the app (You, tap your email under your name, then Delete my account) or, without the app, at vsatlas.app/delete.html with your email and password or a code we email you. Either way it is deleted from Supabase at once, with everything it keeps. An account made with Apple is deleted in the app on your iPhone.

Not medical advice

vs Atlas is for general wellness and education. It doesn’t diagnose or treat any condition and isn’t a substitute for a doctor.

Adults only

vs Atlas is for adults 18 and over. We don’t knowingly collect data from anyone under 18. If you think someone under 18 has an account, email us and we’ll delete it.

Your rights

You can see, correct and delete your data at any time. Your health data is on your phone, where you can change or delete it; a backup file is a copy of everything in the app. The switches under You → your account decide what friends see, and turning one off takes that data off your account. You can delete your account in the app or at vsatlas.app/delete.html. To ask for a copy of what your account keeps, or anything else about your data, email us. If you’re in the EU or the UK you can also complain to your data protection authority.

How long it’s kept

Your account and what it keeps stay until you delete them. Your week’s results are kept for eight weeks, cheers for 30 days and challenge links for 90 days. Medals stay on your account until you delete it. Reports you send are kept while we look into them, and at most a year.

Where it’s processed

Your optional account is stored by Supabase and the sign-in and code emails are sent by Resend, both in the United States, under their data processing terms (including the EU standard contractual clauses). They process it only to run the service for us. We don’t sell your data, don’t share it for advertising and don’t use analytics or tracking.

Why we use it

We use your account data to give you the account, friends and leaderboard features you ask for. Blood results, your photo and record videos are shared only with your consent, given with their switches, and you can take it back at any time by turning a switch off.

Changes

If this policy changes, we’ll update this page and the date above.
